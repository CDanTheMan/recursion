## The question

How do `#general`, dedicated ARM conversations and shared multi-ARM rooms map onto durable actors without creating a hidden executive tier? Topology, addressing and lease semantics determine whether direct Owner-to-ARM conversation ships safely, so the lane asks how a durable actor, the surface used to address it and its current authority stay distinct; how shared rooms resolve conflicts, cycles and cross-goal budgets; how the accepted bounded lease interacts with Head's retained ownership; how transport normalization and recovery work; and whether any public system or study settles the design. The accepted interaction-surfaces boundary (CLI canonical, Telegram optional, no graphical frontend required, direct conversation and bounded coordination leases accepted) is fixed and is not reopened.

## Key findings

- **Evidence basis and scope.** The report claims no Deep Research plugin session and inspected no private repository, transcript, account configuration or operational Recursion instance; the supplied brief is treated as an accepted research constraint, not independently verified runtime behavior, and every consequential public claim is tied to a source id. [B0]
- **A durable logical actor, its addressing surface and its current authority are three different things.** Actor identity, GoalSpec and revision, canonical work/decision state, room and membership revision, route binding, execution attempt and transport credential generation are related but not synonyms. A process restart is not the birth of an executive and a topic deletion is not its death. [B0; P1]
- **A room coordinates a workflow and acquires no portfolio powers.** It owns no goals, holds no pooled budget, cannot admit members and cannot settle cross-goal disputes. Being readable or addressable in a room grants nothing. A speaker selector or summarizer is not an executive; only reachable reserved powers make a second executive appear. [I1; P1; P3]
- **A named ARM may coordinate a bounded step under the already accepted lease.** The lease binds room, workflow step, named holder, participant actor ids, allowed verbs, budget slice, issue/expiry/revocation epochs and a fencing token. The holder's own GoalSpec and each participant's permissions remain ceilings; the holder cannot amend GoalSpecs, add members, transfer the lease, settle cross-goal disputes, retire ARMs or escalate strategically. [B0; P3]
- **Head retains goal ownership, room membership, the authority envelope, conflict resolution, ARM lifecycle and strategic model escalation**, plus independent review/acceptance discipline: a holder's own PASS or a friendly room consensus is never acceptance. [B0]
- **The exact Grok Bot reference is verified against official documentation (G1–G5, retrieved 2026-10-01).** Published behavior supports persistent named conversations, parallel work, explicit addressing, asynchronous wake-and-reply handoffs and visible handoff progress, and advises one owner per stage and warns about duplication/noise from excessive parallel handoffs. [G1–G3; F]
- **The same reference proves no isolation and no authority transfer.** Bots share a user-assigned computer, files, sessions and logins; separate Bots are explicitly not a security boundary; browser and command-line credentials are shared; and stopping does not undo completed actions. Published interaction descriptions do not establish safe authority transfer, fencing or exactly-once effects. [G4, G5; I2]
- **Three imports are rejected:** shared ambient credentials (surface sharing is not capability sharing), the visible transcript as an authority tie-breaker, and unconstrained ownership passing in place of the expiring, nontransferable step lease. [B0]
- **Addressing is kept separate from authority.** A topic, mention, reply, bot username, room title or human-readable role label can supply routing evidence only; it cannot substitute for an authenticated principal plus current authority. A direct Owner instruction stays inside the ARM's existing GoalSpec and cannot silently widen privacy, spending, credentials, publication or deployment scope; an expansion enters the retained Head/Owner path. [B0; P2]
- **Admission needs reconstructable evidence.** The proposed command record carries stable command and intended-operation identity, authenticated issuer, target actor, selected route and binding revision, room and membership revision, GoalSpec identity and revision, requested action and affected resource, causation/supersession references, current grant and lease references, explicit goal-owned budget attribution and disposition. Received, authenticated, routed, admitted, started, effect-outcome-known, independently reviewed and accepted remain distinct states; a delivery receipt proves neither acceptance nor completion. [P2]
- **Lease validity is scoped, not merely time-based.** The scope binds exact room and step, holder, participant set, membership revision and relevant GoalSpec revisions; epochs identify grant and revocation generations, and a logical epoch is not elapsed time. Renewal and reassignment need a new attributable Head-authorized grant; rehydration from an old checkpoint cannot revive a revoked incarnation; expiry does not erase prior evidence, reservations or outcomes. [P3]
- **Three transport topologies are eligible and undecided** — T-A one bot with logical-actor routing, T-B one bot per ARM, T-C hybrid. Bot count is not an isolation proof, and a token authenticates a transport bot rather than the Owner or an ARM; per-ARM tokens narrow that credential's transport scope unless backend privileges, storage and execution boundaries also separate. Each option has a named competitor and rejection tests; none is selected. [T3, T5; I4; P1]
- **Conflicts are classified before they are ordered.** Canonical event order supports reproducible bookkeeping but cannot decide what contradictory instructions mean. Contributions are classified as observation, proposal, authorized command, explicit supersession, review finding or outcome, and tested against current goal and resource revisions; transport arrival order, client time and model confidence never choose the semantic winner. Competing evidence is preserved and the conflicting commitment blocked pending Head or reserved Owner resolution. [B0; P2, P4]
- **Budget ownership is explicit and non-poolable.** Every accepted reservation or expenditure has one authorized cost owner and the relevant goal-owned slice; a room serving two goals holds a vector of slices, not a common purse. Cross-goal allocation remains with Head under the Owner's envelope. A coordination holder cannot borrow spare allowance, refill a slice, move a debit or invent a refund by conversational agreement; an uncertain effect stays unresolved until reconciled. [B0; P4; F10]
- **The central safety finding: a lease record plus expiry time plus fencing-token field is insufficient on its own.** A paused holder must be rejected where the protected commitment is accepted. An upstream check followed by an unfenced downstream request leaves a time-of-check/time-of-use gap, and a generation field that nobody enforces is annotation. A resource that merely remembers the highest token it has seen can keep accepting an old holder before a newer fence reaches it. [A12–A14; I3; P3]
- **Revocation needs more than a request.** Three distinct moments exist: revocation requested, revocation committed in canonical authority state, and revocation effective at the relevant protected boundary. Revocation without a successor exposes the stale-authority problem especially clearly; the acceptance contract must name the effective revocation barrier or current-authority check and the disposition of already-dispatched requests. [I3; P3]
- **Already dispatched effects can remain uncertain.** A crash after an external effect but before acknowledgement leaves the outcome unknown unless reliable reconciliation evidence establishes it, and no report-level design can manufacture a remote idempotency guarantee the effect service does not supply. [A09, A11; P4]
- **Uncertainty may not be reported as success.** A timeout can mean an effect happened; a missing success message need not mean no effect. The report forbids reporting dispatched-but-unknown effects as successful cancellation or zero cost; the truthful state is explicitly unknown followed by bounded reconciliation within existing authority. [P3; P4]
- **Telegram remains an optional projection of canonical state, never canonical state itself.** Dated primary API observations (Bot API 10.3, 24 August 2026; retrieved 2026-10-01): polling and webhooks are mutually exclusive; updates persist at most 24 hours; message IDs are chat-local; `message_thread_id` supports forum supergroups and topic-enabled private bot chats; new and edited messages are separate updates; and no guaranteed outgoing idempotency key or token-rotation queue survival is documented. [T1, T4; F]
- **Bot-to-bot and topic support are conditional and the sources conflict.** Current feature documentation permits bot-to-bot communication under stated mode, admin and privacy conditions, while an older blanket FAQ restriction conflicts with the newer material. The report uses the dated conditional rule, records the conflict and claims no universal delivery. Transport arrival, rate pressure, deletion or token replacement cannot create authority. [T2–T4; B0]
- **Normalization keeps identity layers distinct.** Logical actor, authenticated principal, transport bot identity, route binding, credential generation, canonical command identity and intended-operation identity are separate; a credential string is never an actor key and a display name is never a principal proof. Intake is persisted before advancing the cursor; a semantic edit needs fresh admission; ordinary deletion cannot safely mean cancellation, lease revocation, retirement or audit erasure. [P2, P4]
- **Credential recovery is only one observation.** Replacing a token for the same bot differs from binding a different bot account even when name and icon match, and neither operation creates a new ARM or revives old grants; a successful token check does not prove end-to-end command receipt, correct subscriptions, absence of stale receivers or queue continuity. [P4]
- **Head awareness and Owner digests derive from canonical state, not transcript scraping.** Immediate means processed as a control-relevant event before dependent protected action (no millisecond SLA invented); revocation, conflict checks, independent-review gates and authority changes can never be postponed by digest cadence. A digest generator has no mutation authority; a model summarizer or deterministic scheduler becomes a hidden second executive only if it can exercise reserved powers independently. [B0; P5; E3]
- **Reviewed public systems supply lessons, not the DS-15 contract.** AutoGen, the OpenAI Agents SDK, LangGraph, A2A, Orleans and the coordination primitives (Chubby, Hazelcast, etcd) document actor/addressing, replay, uncertain completion, guardrail-coverage limits and scheduling tradeoffs, but none establishes GoalSpec, retained Head powers or the accepted lease. An actor abstraction does not automatically solve natural-language conflicts, and a checkpoint does not retroactively cancel effects. [A01–A15; I7]
- **Human coordination benefit is unverified.** No located study measures Owner-to-ARM conversation against a matched alternative for total coordination effort, lost context, contradiction rate and review bypass. Five human-agent studies inform plausible mechanisms, measurements and stress cases only; their short tasks, bundled interface changes and simulated failures do not prove a Recursion benefit. [H1–H5; U1]
- **The evaluation design measures realized costs, not preference.** Direct addressing with explicit canonical receipts versus minimal receipts under identical authority and review rules is the primary product-facing comparison; Head-relayed delivery is a research reference only, never a reopened product alternative. The primary effort measure is total active Owner time spent finding and routing work, restating context, reconciling instructions, checking state and repairing errors, aggregated over all attempted tasks alongside independently accepted count, failure count and effort per accepted task; bot topology is compared in a separate matched stage. [P7]
- **A combined comparison that changes interface, agents, latency and permission rules at once cannot identify a direct-addressing effect.** Hold goals, capabilities, GoalSpecs, review requirements and error/difficulty scripts constant; use synthetic projects with known ground truth; counterbalance condition and task order; report training costs and retain failed tasks; and have the Owner predeclare the minimum worthwhile saving and tolerable non-safety tradeoffs before outcomes are examined. A small non-significant comparison is inconclusive, not a pass. [P7]
- **Synthetic fixtures are proposed and all are NOT RUN.** Fixtures F01–F24 cover topology noninterference, direct-address scope, reserved powers, simultaneous conflict, compatible concurrency, retries and repeated intent, paused/expired holders, revocation without a successor, budget ownership, cycles and unavailable Head, independent review, lost projections, and a nonfencing negative control, plus transport/credential/edit/digest families. The bounded target is a noninterference property: with actors, goals, grants and intended commands fixed, changing route topology or presentation order creates no new authority, cost ownership or review bypass. No finite example set proves the universal claim. [P6]

- **The suite needs positive and negative controls.** A suite that rejects everything proves no liveness, so in-scope commands, valid successor grants, authorized Head decisions and genuinely independent review must also succeed under stated assumptions, while the nonfencing resource or an implementer treated as verifier must make it fail. A generated expected transcript, an implementer's own PASS or a mock-adapter pass has no evidentiary value for a different real adapter. [P6]

## Recommendation and falsifier

The report's recommendation is **R1**: evaluate one transport-independent actor and authority contract with attributable direct commands, explicit GoalSpec revisions, current bounded step leases enforced at protected boundaries, canonical Head awareness and independent acceptance. Its named competitor is **R0**: Head-mediated approval of each protected workflow commitment. Both retain direct Owner-to-ARM conversation, bounded coordination leases and the fixed CLI/optional-Telegram boundary, and neither entails a new executive. R1 is a candidate for evaluation, not a topology, protocol or product-default decision. [P1–P7]

- **R1 safety falsifier:** an independently reproducible test in which route remapping grants reserved Head authority, a fresh protected commitment is accepted after the declared effective revocation barrier, or missing independent review is treated as acceptance. The strong fence claim is also unsupported for an effect class that lacks an enforceable boundary.
- **R1 benefit falsifier:** a matched evaluation shows no meaningful reduction in Owner coordination effort, or unacceptable increases in context loss, contradictory instructions or unreviewed work. A conformance pass would not establish human benefit.
- **R0 is a serious competitor but not automatically safe:** a stale Head-approved request can still commit after its authorization changes, so the same effect-boundary reasoning applies to it.
- Free-form ownership transfer and a separate AI ARM Manager are rejected boundary violations, not eligible product alternatives. Sub-proposals P1–P7 (actor/topology contract, attributable admission, enforceable lease, normalization/recovery, canonical digests, independent fixture gate, matched empirical evaluation) each carry a compatible competitor and a decisive falsifier.

## What this does not prove

- No working Recursion system was observed; the consistency conclusions and acceptance conditions are proposals, and the synthetic fixtures were not executed.
- The Grok Bot pattern proves persistent named conversations, parallel work and addressing at the level of published documentation, not operational isolation, safe authority transfer, fencing or exactly-once effects.
- Telegram findings are dated documentation observations, not service guarantees; no configured bot, receiver, admin/privacy combination, topic behavior, credential rotation or backlog recovery was tested, so no SLA or universal delivery is inferred.
- The lease/fencing analysis follows a stated failure model; it does not prove universal fencing support, immediate revocation at every external effect boundary, or that every in-flight effect can be canceled.
- No evidence establishes that direct conversation reduces Owner effort, nor that any topology is more usable or better isolated; topology advantages remain hypotheses to be measured under identical authority rules.
- Public orchestration systems cannot certify Recursion authority semantics, and an actor abstraction or repeated-summary consensus does not resolve natural-language conflicts.

## Open Owner decisions

- What transport-compromise containment is acceptable, and which genuine privacy or capability domains merit separate transport identities (informs, but does not select, T-A/T-B/T-C).
- Which protected-effect classes a bounded step lease may coordinate within the existing envelope, and the acceptable delay between requested and effective revocation for each.
- The authenticated sender-to-Owner binding and supported message classes acceptable for optional Telegram use; unsupported contexts must remain explicit.
- Semantic-edit, explicit-cancellation, retention and redaction rules for canonical evidence, keeping presentation deletion distinct from authority revocation.
- Budget attribution for legitimate multi-goal work, and the no-progress conditions that require escalation rather than a room-invented allocation rule.
- Consequence windows warranting immediate Owner notice, plus digest cadence and evidence detail that avoids copying private ARM content unnecessarily.
- The minimum worthwhile coordination saving and tolerable non-safety tradeoffs; acceptance still requires independent review and the fixed authority constraints.

## Relevance to later waves

- Supplies the actor/surface/authority separation, the T-A/T-B/T-C topology comparison frame, the addressing-versus-authority admission contract, the bounded-lease enforcement requirements, the canonical conflict, budget and digest contracts, and the F01–F24 fixture families as a testable design.
- Later waves should treat R1 and R0 as competitors to evaluate, not as decisions, and keep direct Owner-to-ARM conversation, Head retention, bounded coordination leases, no new executive tier and the CLI/optional-Telegram boundary fixed.
- The safety rule that uncertain dispatched effects must never be reported as successful cancellation or zero cost, and that missing verification is never a pass, carries into every later intake and verification lane.
