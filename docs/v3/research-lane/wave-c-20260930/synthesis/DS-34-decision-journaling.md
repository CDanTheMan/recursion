## The question

How should a low-volume individual decision log be structured and reviewed so operating decisions are scored against outcomes predicted before the outcome is known rather than reconstructed by hindsight — reconciling with Recursion's existing D-entry convention (falsifier on every decision, monthly first-of-month review) instead of creating a parallel log, and naming which calibration claims the evidence does and does not support? [B0]

## Key findings

**(a) Three separations.** A **forecast outcome is not a decision falsifier**: a 70% event can fail to occur without disproving that probability, while a precommitted threshold can fire and require review. **A decision assessment is not an execution command**: a fired falsifier creates evidence and an escalation obligation, not authority to reverse an Owner direction or change the constitution. **A favorable outcome is not proof of a good process**: process review uses entry-available information; outcome scoring uses the frozen resolution rule. [I1, S34]

**(b) Calibration evidence is bounded.** Tournaments make accuracy measurable when questions are resolvable, probabilities are recorded before resolution, and a declared score spans many outcomes (`S1`, `S2`, `S33`). Transfer to individual project decisions is an inference with conditions: stable definitions, honest entry times, observable resolution, a horizon, and enough comparable cases. A person who predicts and also intervenes differs from a passive forecaster; tournament evidence cannot identify the counterfactual under an unchosen architecture. [S1–S4; I1]

**(c) The tournament-training dispute.** A 2025 reanalysis models question selection and timing as well as latent ability; adjusted training/team effects were reduced, eliminated, or sometimes reversed (`S3`) — exploratory and not preregistered. A 2025 adversarial-collaboration proposal disputes treating strategy as nuisance and proposes common questions/times (`S4`); it is a proposal, not a result. [S3, S4]

**(d) Low-volume measures.** The preferred primary probability measure is the binary Brier score `(p − y)²` on a declared eligible cohort with its denominator; unknown outcomes score nothing. Accuracy, calibration, resolution, and sharpness are distinct (`S5`–`S7`). At low volume avoid binned reliability diagrams, expected calibration error, estimated decomposition terms, calibration regressions, significance stars, or league tables as stable evidence; finite-sample decomposition bias is established (`S6`) and stable reliability diagrams still need inference assumptions (`S28`). There is **no universal case count** at which a personal log becomes calibrated, and repeated updates to one question remain one resolved event. Interval/quantile targets need a matching score and explicit functional; interval scores penalize width and misses (`S29`). [S5–S7, S28, S29; I2, I3]

**(e) Feedback is mixed, not a monotonic learning curve.** Primary studies (`S8`, `S19`–`S24`) show: most early gain followed a large first feedback batch with limited transfer; calibration feedback differed from plain outcome feedback; feedback can improve discrimination while worsening overconfidence; a multiyear tournament did not establish calibration improvement over time; two large 2025 experiments found no calibration gain from the tested feedback; a software lessons-learned study did not outperform controls. No source establishes that one person becomes calibrated after one month, ten entries, or three reviews, and no calendar-to-cases conversion is justified. [S8, S19–S24; I2]

**(f) Expertise vs. forecasting skill.** Technical knowledge and probabilistic judgment solve different problems. Expert intuition is most defensible where regularities exist and informative feedback is available; software effort-prediction intervals can be overconfident (`S25`). The report recommends a domain-informed forecast with an independently checkable resolver, not an authority-weighted confidence label. [S2, S9, S25]

**(g) Journal formats.** Practitioner decision journals and architecture decision records document context/options/expectations and context/decision/status/consequences (`S10`, `S11`); published templates describe intended behavior but supply no adherence or comparative-cadence evidence (`U1`).

**(g′) The reviewable minimum.** The seven supplied concepts (decision, options, choice, rationale, expected outcome, falsifier, review trigger) are necessary; to be executable they need identity, entry time, authority reference, evidence, a responsible reviewer, an observable target with a horizon, and a resolver. Non-quantifiable choices record a qualitative expectation and are marked probability-ineligible with a reason; a missing probability is unknown, never 0% or 50%. [S10, S11; R1a, R1b]

**(h) Graded outcomes, compliance, bias.** Predefine exhaustive, mutually exclusive categories or a numeric target with frozen boundaries; use multicategory Brier or ranked probability scores, not invented numeric distances for labels (`S7`). Compliance warning signs: boilerplate falsifiers, checkbox resolution, retrospective probabilities, unreasoned rescheduling; proposed controls are an entry admissibility check, due-review coverage, evidence-linked resolutions, and a burden audit. Hindsight and outcome bias are documented (`S15`–`S18`): freeze entry values, recover the record before scoring, apply the frozen resolver, and assess process using only entry-available information. Vagueness is caught at entry by an independent reader classifying a clearly passing, clearly failing, and boundary case from the entry alone. [S7, S15–S18; I4]

**(i) Reference classes and base rates.** Choose the class before learning the result; define unit, start/end events, horizon, population, and inclusion characteristics (`S12`, `S13`). Outside-view prompts reduced directional optimism without demonstrated absolute accuracy gain (`S14`). Historical software-estimation errors can inform intervals but do not validate a small local tail (`S26`); historical-error prompts widened intervals without established accuracy (`S27`). "No usable class found" is an informative state, not zero risk. Record outside view, inside-view adjustment, and final forecast separately, preserving the unadjusted forecast. The beta-binomial example is conditional arithmetic, not a measured local rate. [S12–S14, S26, S27; I3]

**(j) Preservation and deterministic flags.** Amendments/supersessions are new dated addenda linked to the original D identifier; original text, reasoning, options, falsifiers, and dates are never edited, renumbered, or backfilled. Assessment (open/held/failed/unclear), authority disposition (current/amended/superseded), and monitoring lifecycle (active/closed) are recorded separately; closure is not a favorable verdict; supersession is not a forecast outcome. Model-free flags over an explicit as-of instant: `MISSING_FALSIFIER`, `INVALID_REVIEW_DATE`, `REVIEW_DUE`, `REVIEW_OVERDUE`, `FALSIFIER_FIRED`, `FALSIFIER_UNEVALUABLE`, `RESOLUTION_PENDING`, `REVIEW_DISPUTED`. The smallest requested check is `MISSING_FALSIFIER OR REVIEW_OVERDUE`, with `INVALID_REVIEW_DATE` so undated records cannot escape; free-text semantic adequacy yields needs-review, not a pass or a model call. [B0; R1a, R1e, R1f, R1g]

**(k) Cadence.** The candidate policy combines a monthly first-of-month time backstop (inherited from Recursion's convention, efficacy unverified) with event triggers: entry gate, outcome time, falsifier event, closure event, assumption/threshold change, and evidence repair. "Immediate" means flagging on observation, not continuous monitoring or new execution authority; no reviewed study validates these exact timings. [B0; R1f]

**(l) Schema shape.** Required field groups conceptually mapped onto a D-entry cover identity/provenance, authority/scope, decision and choice, options, rationale and evidence cutoff, expected outcome, forecast and eligibility, resolution contract, falsifier, review obligation, baseline status, scoring plan, and current projection; conditional/optional groups cover reference-class detail, graded boundaries, intervals, source freshness, material interventions, supersession, utility, sensitivity, and analogies. A required review record carries its own identifier, original D reference, reviewer/time, fulfilled obligation, frozen forecast version, evidence and as-of time, resolved outcome or explicit unknown, falsifier evaluation, score or ineligibility reason, process assessment, authority disposition/lifecycle, next authorized action, next due obligation, and any reschedule reason with the original due time. Historical omissions are marked unavailable, never backfilled as though known at the original date. [B0; R1a, R1b]

**(m) The worked examples are synthetic.** The binary, ordered-category, interval, and overdue/superseded examples are invented illustrations with locally checked arithmetic (for example a miss at p=0.70 scores Brier 0.49 against a 0.50 baseline's 0.25; multicategory 0.78 unnormalized or 0.39 normalized; ranked probability 0.29 or 0.145; an interval score of 28 days). They demonstrate semantics only — not measured Recursion rates, not calibration evidence, and not D-017. [S7, S29; I3]

**(n) Accounting discipline.** Keep a prospective eligible-case inventory including abandoned and unresolved cases; count distinct events and projects; report unresolved cases, cancellations, disputed outcomes, and exclusions. Measure due-review completion, outstanding-obligation age, missing resolution evidence, and time burden as process measures only — never as calibration or decision quality. [R1c, R1d, R1f; I2]

## Recommendation and falsifier

The report recommends **R1**: a prospective forecast-and-review extension to the existing D-entry convention, subject to Owner acceptance and independent compatibility review — keeping the monthly first-of-month review and adding explicit outcome dates and event triggers, a small set of resolution fields, and append-only review/amendment records associated with the original D-entry. It explicitly does **not** create a second decision log, reopen accepted Owner directions, or edit existing D history. Its immediate justification is auditability and timely review (an independent reviewer can recover what was expected, why, which observation would undermine it, and whether the promised review happened); improved calibration is a testable downstream hypothesis, not a promised gain. [R1, R1a–R1g]

The **competing option R0** is the present narrative D-entry convention with its existing falsifier and monthly review, without the structured fields: lower entry burden, less directly testable reconstruction. [R1]

The **decisive falsifier** for R1's auditability claim is one materially consequential eligible pilot record for which an independent reviewer cannot recover the original expectation, falsifier, resolution contract, and review status without inventing or silently changing entry-time information; that failure requires repair or simplification before calling the schema reviewable (it does not authorize rewriting the historical entry). For operational usefulness the Owner should predeclare an acceptable entry/review burden, a review-service target, and a prospective evaluation window, and compare R1 with R0 on the same kinds of decisions and all due obligations; if R1 exceeds the burden budget without improving the agreed reconstructability or timely-review criterion, reject or reduce the added fields. Few Brier scores must not be the adoption pass condition. [R1]
