## The question

Can Recursion support a **scientific-research ARM** that finds papers and standards, preserves provenance, reconciles contradictory evidence, designs reproducible experiments, and produces defensible architecture claims without violating privacy, copyright, access, or budget? The brief requires primary/official evidence, typed claim–source relations, explicit treatment of `unknown`, independent verification, adversarial testing, and a lean kernel with scholarly providers and heavyweight analysis tooling as optional plugins.

## Key findings

- **Feasible, but not "search → summarize → cite."** [Inferred] The strongest candidate is an **authority-first federated evidence layer**: registration/domain authorities for identity, version, and editorial state; aggregators for discovery and graph enrichment; full text as a separately licensed artifact; provider-native provenance preserved; and every material source-state change forced back through claim adjudication.
- **Five different questions.** Identity, access, editorial state, scientific entailment, and local architectural fit are distinct. A resolving DOI does not prove currency; a current paper does not prove a claim; a result in one population/workload does not transfer; and an external mechanism paper cannot prove the mechanism improves Recursion without local structural and preregistered empirical evidence.
- **Registration/authority surfaces.** [Source-documented, high confidence] Crossref's production REST API provides DOI metadata, licenses, and post-publication updates (public/polite/Plus limits 5/10/150 per interval, concurrency 1/3/unlimited); Retraction Watch data is integrated in production while the old Labs integration is stale and must not be a current-status source. DataCite exposes DOIs and explicit version/supersession relations, but public retrieval excludes Registered/Draft records, so **absence from the public API is not evidence of nonexistence or retraction**. PubMed models retracted-publication and retraction-notice types plus errata and linked corrections; E-utilities asks for tool/email and an API key above three calls/second. Europe PMC v6.9 exposes status-update search and OA-subset full text, but the reviewed page stated no numeric rate limit, so it remains `unknown`. arXiv exposes first-submission versus retrieved-version dates and optional DOI/journal links, asks a three-second inter-call delay, and prefers OAI-PMH for bulk. Standards status is body-specific (IETF Datatracker, W3C dated/latest, NIST final/draft/withdrawn, ISO edition/lifecycle).
- **Aggregators are discovery layers, not authorities.** [Source-documented] OpenAlex offers cross-provider identifiers, references, copy-level version/license/provenance, and a retraction flag, but its access model is changing (polite-pool behavior retired) with active deprecations. Semantic Scholar supplies search, graph, bulk/dataset, and recommendation capability, but its licensing/request policy requires independent admission review. Derived aggregation cannot alone satisfy the hard floor for current editorial status or source-specific rights.
- **Full-text rights are a hard boundary, not an annotation.** [Source-documented] A Crossref full-text link does not establish access rights; arXiv metadata is CC0 but its default article license does not generally grant third-party redistribution; PMC requires approved automated retrieval plus per-article license checks; ISO publications are copyright-protected and current policy restricts AI-tool use absent applicable permission. Rights attach to the **exact artifact/location**, because the same work can have publisher, accepted-manuscript, and submitted versions with different licenses.
- **Operation ladder.** Ask separately whether an artifact may be discovered, identified, retrieved, temporarily processed, persisted, quoted, embedded/indexed, or redistributed/published. Unknown rights mean the operation is **not authorized**, not "probably open"; a snippet/abstract cannot be silently substituted for an unavailable argument.
- **Source lifecycle is a state machine.** [Inferred from documented mechanisms] Discovered → Identified → RetrievedVerified, with branches to Quarantined, Inaccessible, Expired, Corrected, Superseded, Retracted, and Withdrawn. Any transition is an `updates` event that enqueues dependent claims for re-adjudication and must be **append-only history**, not destructive replacement, so a verifier can reconstruct what evidence was available at an earlier decision.
- **Retraction is an event with multiple observations.** [Source-documented] The same event may be represented more than once (Crossref/RW and PubMed), so duplicate notices must never count as independent scientific evidence.
- **Preprint-to-journal linking.** [Source-documented/Inferred] Do not collapse records on fuzzy title/author matching; prefer a verified identifier or provider relation (arXiv DOI/journal reference, Crossref/DataCite relations). Fuzzy matching may propose a pair; only a verified relation or independently reviewed metadata match establishes the normalized relationship.
- **Lean kernel schemas.** [Inferred, on a W3C PROV foundation] `SourceRecord` carries internal identity, persistent IDs, minimum bibliography, version/time, distinct source/editorial states with append-only observation history, retrieval provenance (adapter id/revision, request fingerprint, response digest, normalized metadata digest), artifact-specific rights, relations, locators, privacy class/route constraints, revalidation triggers, and a lossless provider-payload artifact. `ResearchClaim` carries one falsifiable proposition, claim type, goal/decision scope, a structured applicability tuple (population, mechanism, comparator, endpoint, workload, time/version), assumptions, evidence edges with exact locators and reviewer rationale, adjudication state, ordinal confidence with rationale, linked experiments, review/independence metadata, history, and expiry. The kernel contains **no** `citation_count_is_quality` field, no model-generated truth score, and no single `is_valid` Boolean.
- **Typed edge vocabulary.** [Inferred from DS-14] `supports`, `contradicts`, `qualifies`, `replicates`, `updates`, `mentions` (discovery only; cannot satisfy support), `metadata-only` (cannot prove a substantive claim), and `inapplicable` (mandatory mismatch reason). The adjudicator normalizes applicability **first** and partitions evidence into commensurable strata; differing populations/endpoints/workloads may imply heterogeneity rather than contradiction, and absent sufficiently direct verified evidence the answer is `unknown` — never a citation-count plurality.
- **Extraction is measurement, not revelation.** [Inferred, on a W3C PROV foundation] Preserve source PID/version, digest, retrieval time and access state; parser identity/version/configuration; page/section/paragraph and bounding box; table coordinates and raw cell strings; figure identifiers; original plus normalized numeric values with units and uncertainty; an explicit OCR flag (never silently merged with native text); and whether a human compared extraction to the rendering. "The parser extracted 17.2 from cell X" is distinct from "17.2 is the true measured effect."
- **Three evidence bands.** [Project requirement from DS-14] External mechanism evidence establishes only that a mechanism/result/standard exists in the cited context; local structural evidence establishes Recursion's contracts/source/tests possess the prerequisite; empirical fit evidence establishes behavior/performance under a preregistered local workload. A permissible final architecture claim names all three; omitting a local layer must narrow the conclusion.
- **Preregistered evaluation — no results asserted.** The report specifies, but did not run, a hard-floor corpus whose primary rule is that **any accepted fabricated/nonexistent identity, retracted-as-current source, stale superseded source, inaccessible content represented as verified full text, inapplicable evidence represented as direct support, or unverifiable claim is an automatic failure**. Implementer and verifier are separated; negative/null findings are reported exactly like positive ones; temporal replay exposes mutable API/pagination/status behavior. Reproduction has offline replay, online revalidation, and restricted-source modes; the spec is to be serialized and content-hashed as its protocol identifier before execution.
- **Adversarial fixtures.** Designed (without fabricated real-looking IDs): false identifiers, near-match metadata, non-entailing sources, retractions, corrections, outdated versions, preprint/journal duplicates, paywalls, contradictory studies, inapplicable populations, heterogeneous endpoints, citation rings, prompt-injected metadata, hostile tables, mutable pagination, license conflicts, negative/null results, and provider deprecation.
- **Lean plugin boundary.** [Inferred] The kernel owns `SourceRecord`/`ResearchClaim` interfaces, lifecycle/revalidation scheduling, the rights/privacy gate, digest interfaces, claim-edge validation, audit history, and independent-review state; provider adapters, parser/OCR, embeddings/stores, and scientific runtimes are lazy plugins. A disabled-path acceptance test (kernel loads with providers/parsers absent, no sockets, no auth, no background indexer) is local empirical evidence documentation cannot supply.
- **Review routing and cost control.** Specialist verifier routing is a bounded capability, not a new executive tier. Cost is bounded at the request layer (rate limits, backoff, caching, pagination caps), the research layer (preregistered provider/request/dollar budgets, diminishing-return stop), and the decision layer (spend only on sources surviving identity, status, relevance, rights gating). No private project name, prompt, unpublished finding, credential, or infrastructure identifier should enter a public scholarly query.

## Recommendation and falsifier

**Recommended candidate: Option A — authority-first federation with aggregator-assisted discovery.** Crossref and DataCite are candidate PID/registration anchors; PubMed/Europe PMC are specialist biomedical authority layers; arXiv is the source for its own preprint/version records; standards are verified against the issuing body; OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar are optional discovery/graph/reconciliation plugins; every full-text artifact passes a separate rights gate. This is explicitly **not an adoption decision**; no provider was promoted to a default.

- **Option B (aggregator-first)** — simpler, fewer adapters, but the hard floor leans on derived aggregation and one provider's changing economics/deprecations; falsified if any hard-floor case admits a stale/retracted/misattributed source an authority adapter would have blocked.
- **Option C (snapshot/bulk-first)** — strong offline reproducibility and bounded large-scale search, but risks staleness; falsified by a material rate of stale status/version decisions that timely online checks would prevent.
- **Option A's falsifier** — if B or C also achieves zero prohibited false acceptances while materially outperforming A on coverage, update latency, verifier reproducibility, and bounded cost, A loses its rationale.

Related recommendations each carry a competing option and falsifier: keep generic schemas in the kernel rather than provider models; store provider-native responses as artifacts rather than expanding the schema; model rights per artifact/location (already justified by documented arXiv/PMC location-level realities); re-adjudicate dependents on status/version change (falsified only if dependency analysis proves the change cannot affect proposition, locator, applicability, or confidence); return `unknown`/heterogeneous for non-comparable evidence (falsified only by a preregistered domain-specific synthesis method that validly transforms unlike studies into a common estimand and passes specialist review); use body-native standards verification (falsified if an aggregator demonstrates complete, current, provenance-preserving status/edition/errata/supersession parity); and treat extraction output as derived observation (falsified only by validation establishing the required error bound for the exact corpus/parser/layout class). The separation of external mechanism, local structure, and empirical fit is a **governing DS-14 hard floor**, falsifiable only by an Owner-level change to the acceptance rule.

## What this does not prove

- Official documentation does not prove actual coverage, uptime, latency, local implementation quality, or Recursion fit; the decisive missing evidence is empirical.
- No live provider trial, Recursion implementation experiment, benchmark, or hard-floor corpus run was authorized or performed; no experiment result or benchmark number is asserted.
- The recommendation is not an adapter adoption decision; every provider remains a **candidate only**.
- A rich citation graph does not prove entailment or editorial authority; provider provenance is evidence metadata, not scientific support.
- "Not found" is never automatically "does not exist" (DataCite visibility is state-dependent), and Europe PMC's rate ceiling remains explicitly `unknown`, not "unlimited."
- ISO full-text model ingestion is blocked absent applicable permission; publication is not authorized merely because a researcher can view a copy.
- Nothing is adopted, deployed, or written to a protected path; no private or paid resource was accessed.

## Open Owner decisions

- The acceptable maximum false-negative rate after the absolute zero-tolerance false-acceptance floor (determines federation breadth).
- Whether authenticated membership-only metadata (e.g., hidden DataCite states) is admissible for verifier workflows, or acceptance must be reproducible through anonymous public access.
- Whether a verifier may rely on lawfully retained restricted artifacts or must independently re-retrieve every evidence-bearing source.
- The exact rights operations Recursion needs (transient reading, persistent storage, extraction, embeddings, short quotation, redistribution, publication).
- Whether ISO metadata-only support is sufficient for v3 given current copyright/AI restrictions.
- Whether the first implementation is domain-neutral or biomedical-first.
- The revalidation service-level objective for high-impact claims.
- Which specialist-review classes are mandatory before a claim may affect architecture.
- What monetary/request budget constitutes a normal research goal.
- Whether every accepted packet must support complete offline replay, or metadata/status online replay suffices for some classes.

## Relevance to later waves

This lane is a blocking prerequisite for any research/evidence capability: the `SourceRecord`/`ResearchClaim` kernel, the rights/privacy gate, the source lifecycle state machine, verification routing, and the research cost ledger. Later waves should consume the report as a **candidate architecture and a specification, not an implemented system**: serialize the preregistration packet as an immutable content-hashed artifact, run Option A against the hard-floor corpus with implementer and verifier separated, compare A/B/C on coverage, update latency, verifier reproduction, and bounded cost, and demonstrate the disabled-path acceptance test locally before any provider adapter or parser is admitted. The DS-14 hard floor — no accepted fabricated, retracted-as-current, stale, misrepresented, inapplicable, or unverifiable evidence, and no local-fit claim without local structural and preregistered empirical evidence — carries forward unchanged.
