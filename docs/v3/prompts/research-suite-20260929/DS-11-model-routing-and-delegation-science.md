## Purpose

Decide which models, harnesses, delegation shapes and verifier combinations maximize accepted outcomes per quota, cost, resource and owner-review unit for each task class, and whether routing needs task-specific route suites or one universal capability ladder. This blocks the v3 routing policy, admission envelopes and the product claim that a strong ARM plus deliberate delegation beats uncontrolled swarms. It is blocking because no comparable local measurement of delegation shape, planning strength or version drift exists yet.

## Research questions

1. Given a contamination-aware corpus spanning planning, architecture, security, backend, frontend, mechanical work, debugging, integration, documentation and research, what minimum sample per task class can distinguish delegation shapes at a pre-registered quality floor?
2. Does one strong ARM with bounded specialist delegation beat multi-ARM collaboration and uncontrolled swarms on first-review acceptance and repair cycles per owner-review minute?
3. Are task-specific route suites measurably better than a single universal capability ladder, and on which task classes does the ladder lose?
4. Does same-route continuity beat checkpoint switching on completion and intent recall, and what are the token, latency and repair costs of switching?
5. Does strong-model planning improve accepted outcome per quota unit over cheap-model planning when implementation and verification routes are held constant?
6. What run-to-run variance exists per task class, and how many repeated attempts are needed before a route comparison is decisive rather than anecdotal?
7. How do repair work, false-green gate rate and owner review time scale with delegation shape and ARM count, and is any growth superlinear?
8. How fast does provider or version drift invalidate a measured ranking, and what revalidation trigger keeps a route policy honest?
9. Which verifier combinations (same-model, same-family, different-family, different-provider) reduce escaped defects per risk tier?
10. Under the selected Sol-director, eight-Luna and two-Terra ceiling, how much duplicated context, same-family blind spot and residual quota loss appears, and does synthesis stay within budget?
11. When is a smaller pre-registered sample decisive enough to cancel a full alternative campaign, and what stopping rule prevents rerunning an expensive design?
12. Which telemetry counters (gate success, verifier disagreement, human correction, provider usage) are actually observable per route, and which must stay unknown rather than zero?
13. Can a task-class route policy stay explainable to the owner (chosen route, rejected alternatives, quality floor, quota reason) without invoking a model?
