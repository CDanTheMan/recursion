# Composition model specification, 2026-09-08

This is a prospective finite subsystem specification, derived from the accepted generation-authority dossier and architecture challenge. No new upstream implementation claim is made. Source inspection remains pinned in those preserved records. Recursion owns the contract and this experimental code; no upstream repository fork or runtime dependency is selected.

Compare two independently implemented transition systems under identical finite inputs: a split authority plus broker/environment record and an inclusive record. Check initialization and every concrete transition against an independently written aggregate relation. The aggregate must expose every fact that changes authority, resource accounting or continuity; only staging and partial physical deletion details may stutter when their visible obligations remain unchanged. Compare complete normalized concrete edge sets as a stronger construction cross-check, not as the definition of semantic equivalence.

Start with one preserved G1 fork, one live local invocation pin on G1 and one already-issued epoch1 remote permit for stable operation O/payload P. The remote operation does not depend on local plugin residency after the owned local process has exited and been waited. That explicit assumption does not cover an in-process blocked host call. Three generation identities share a cap of two resident generations, including prepared/staged candidates. Compare a durable history capsule with a policy retaining the fork's resident generation. History capsule count/size is separately bounded to one immutable synthetic unit; it is not free or unbounded storage.

A candidate has prepare, stage, publish-commit and acknowledgment/lost-reply stages. Selection changes only at commit with a current writer term. One authority restart advances the term, retains committed selection/pins/retirement and invalidates outstanding writer grants. A new writer can reauthorize an existing staged candidate. This slice has two authority terms and deliberately omits same-term permit serials, which remain required by the prior probe. For each old generation, retirement is durable intent, first deletion is separate, and final reclamation releases residency. Delete only after no applicable live invocation pin and no resident-only fork dependency. Reclamation is not retirement of a receiver completion record.

A G3 request remains recorded. When capacity is full, status visibly reports hold; Stop and status remain available. After capsule commit and applicable local return or owned kill/wait, G1 can be reclaimed and G3 can become active. A resident-only preserved fork may prevent that transition indefinitely. Neither policy is selected for production by this comparison. Permanent remote nonresponse is permitted. We establish safety and existential progress witnesses only; no fairness or eventual completion theorem is claimed in this packet.

Local Stop advances admission epoch1 to2. Recipient installation is a separate event. The old in-flight permit may execute before installation, including after local Stop; report that exposure. Once installed, the same permit is rejected. Operation completion is independent of local process death. A retained authenticated receipt for O/P may classify the effect as known despite Stop or a new local incarnation; wrong operation/payload/authentication must be rejected. This is an abstract authentication predicate, not production authentication or a literal M1A/M1C join. Stop here is task revocation, not operational PAUSE. No model action establishes permission to append M1A observations under PAUSE.

Permit at most one remote effect execution. A second local incarnation is a reconciliation/query attempt, never a retry of an unresolved external effect. Track each of the two issued attempts and its closed status separately, with a two-unit lifetime allowance. One provider usage-reset event must not erase issued segments. Old transport delivery acknowledgment does not close the new attempt. Local attempt closure and external operation uncertainty remain separate. A valid late original receipt can resolve O while attempt2 remains open until its own completion. Fork inspection after Stop preserves content but cannot reactivate its old grant.

The finite domain is three generations, two resident slots, one fork/capsule, one local G1 invocation, one operation/payload, one delayed permit, two typed admission/recipient epochs, two writer terms, two local attempt incarnations, one transport ACK, one reset and one pending publication reply. No hidden event/depth bound or identity symmetry. Self-loops represent rejected/status/inspection/query events. Explore all reachable states to an empty frontier within the committed limits; a limit is an incomplete result. The aggregate projection hides publication stage and partial deletion only, reports duplicate projections, and retains complete internal witness labels.

## Method challenge refinement before the second execution

The first committed model run completed both graphs but its restart witness allowed restart before publication. Preserve that run as superseded. Add ordered publish→restart→lost-reply and first-delete→restart→final-reclaim traces, plus a single complete retention/revocation/late-receipt path. An independent broker-edge relation now checks stage-before-publish, first-delete-before-reclaim and every change to hidden broker fields. Add missing-stage and skipped-first-delete mutants: matching two reducers alone cannot certify these guards. Wrong-operation, wrong-payload and unauthenticated input classes have separate refusal controls after a completed effect. These are explicit finite environment labels, not cryptographic verification. The fresh receipt positive is for the initially current epoch1 permit; issuance of a fresh epoch2 permit remains outside this one-permit domain.

Status is independently checked: G3 capacity hold requires a recorded request, selection below G3, no candidate already being prepared, and both resident slots occupied. A missing hold label must fail even if both concrete reducers omit it.
